# NetLab — Topic Mapping

How the four college Data Communication PDFs map onto NetLab, and how the existing lessons are preserved.

- **College syllabus topics** (41) live under **Unit I – Unit IV** and are written from the PDF slides.
- **Additional Learning Topics** (24) are the original NetLab lessons, kept unchanged with the same ids, so saved progress, quiz scores and links still work.
- Every topic follows the same workflow: **Theory → Visualize → Simulate → Quiz → Notes**.

Slide numbers refer to the page numbers of the PDFs.

## Accuracy notes on the slides

Where a slide is wrong or misleading, the lesson keeps the slide's wording as the expected exam answer and adds a **Syllabus note** with the technically correct statement:

| Slide | Slide says | Correct statement |
|---|---|---|
| Unit I, 20 | Serial "is not affected by noise", "bandwidth is higher" | Serial links are still affected by noise. They avoid crosstalk and skew *between* parallel wires, which is why they can be clocked much faster. |
| Unit I, 40 | Dial modem "56000 per/sec" | 56 kbit/s downstream (V.90/V.92); upstream is 33.6–48 kbit/s. |
| Unit II, 12 | Coax has "2 parallel conductors" | The two conductors are **concentric** (they share one axis). |
| Unit II, 14 | Core surrounded by "the coating" | The core is surrounded by the **cladding** (lower refractive index); the coating/buffer is the protective layer outside it. |
| Unit II, 17 | Stripline "is also called a waveguide" | Stripline is a TEM transmission line, not a hollow waveguide. |
| Unit II, 22 | Microwave distance "directly proportional to antenna height" | Line-of-sight range grows with the **square root** of height: d ≈ 3.57(√h₁ + √h₂) km. |
| Unit II, 25 | Infrared "300 GHz to 400 GHz" | 300 GHz to 400 **THz** (slide 24 gives this correctly). |
| Unit II, 32 | "4G offers CDMA"; 5G "OFDM, BDMA" | 4G LTE uses OFDMA (downlink) and SC-FDMA (uplink); CDMA was 3G. 5G NR uses OFDM/OFDMA. ITU peak targets: 4G 1 Gbit/s, 5G 20 Gbit/s down / 10 Gbit/s up. |
| Unit III, 64 | Amplitude jitter from "power supply noise (60 Hz)" | 60 Hz is the US mains frequency; in India the mains frequency is 50 Hz. |
| Unit IV, 5 | Modulation "increases strength" | Modulation shifts information onto a carrier at a suitable frequency; amplifiers increase strength. |
| Unit IV, 10 | PM example "'1' = 0°, '0' = 180°" | That example is BPSK (digital). Analog PM varies phase continuously with the message. |
| Unit IV, 21 | QPSK "decreases the data bit rate to half" | For the same bit rate, QPSK **halves the symbol (baud) rate**, so it needs about half the bandwidth of BPSK. |
